Infrared Fitness

Where to Find the Best Infrared Yoga and Pilates in the Algarve

Infrared heat changes how yoga and pilates feel. Here is where to find it in the Algarve, how it works, and what to expect.

What infrared heat actually does

Infrared heat works differently from the forced-air heat in a traditional hot yoga studio. Instead of warming the air around you, infrared panels emit electromagnetic radiation that penetrates the skin and warms the body directly. The result is heat that feels deeper, gentler, and less suffocating.

The practical differences are clear: you do not leave with a dry throat. You do not feel like you are fighting the air to breathe. Your muscles warm from the inside, which makes them more pliable without the oppressive humidity of traditional hot yoga.

Infrared studios typically run at 28 to 32 degrees Celsius, compared to 38 to 40 degrees in traditional hot yoga. The lower air temperature means you can work harder without overheating, and the heat continues to penetrate during static holds like Yin yoga poses.

Infrared yoga and pilates in the Algarve: the honest picture

As of 2026, there is one dedicated infrared-heated studio in the Algarve that offers regular yoga and pilates classes: Heat Lagos. Several wellness spaces offer infrared saunas and heat therapies, but Heat Lagos is the only place where infrared heat is integrated into the actual class format.

That does not mean there are no other heat-based options in the region. It means you should know what you are booking before you arrive. This guide separates true infrared classes from other heat experiences.

1. Heat Lagos - the only infrared-heated studio in the Algarve

Location: Lagos old town, western Algarve.

What they offer: Infrared-heated yoga, pilates, sculpt, and recovery classes. The panels are Sunlighten infrared units mounted on the ceiling and walls. They heat the room to around 30 degrees Celsius while maintaining normal humidity.

What makes it different: Most yoga studios in the Algarve focus on spiritual, traditional styles. Most pilates studios focus on slow reformer work. Very few offer genuinely challenging, modern fitness classes. Heat Lagos does. Sculpt is the most popular class - a hardcore fitness session combining pilates, strength training, and high reps in the infrared heat. You will feel it for two days. For something mellower, Heat Flow and Heat Pilates are the options. Heat Yin is very popular for winding down and recovery.

Class types:

  • Heat Sculpt: The most popular class. A hardcore fitness session - pilates, strength training, and high reps in the infrared heat. You will feel it for two days.
  • Heat Power: Demanding heated yoga with strength elements. The heat raises cardiovascular demand without overwhelming you.
  • Heat Flow: Heated Vinyasa. A more mellow option - movement without intensity.
  • Heat Yin: Long holds in the warmth. Very popular for winding down. The panels keep releasing tension into fascia while you stay still.
  • Heat Pilates: Mat pilates in the warmth. A mellow but effective core session.
  • Heat Recovery: Gentle mobility in the warmth. Designed for active bodies that need release, not intensity.
  • Heat Mobility: Targeted joint and muscle work. Good for maintaining range of motion between surf or training sessions.

Who it is for: Active people - surfers, runners, cyclists, gym-goers - who want the benefits of heat without the aggression of traditional hot yoga. Also excellent for beginners because the heat makes muscles more pliable and the teachers offer modifications for every level.

2. Algarve Alchemy - infrared sauna, not infrared yoga

Location: Lagos old town, above Black and White Cafe.

What they offer: Algarve Alchemy has an infrared sauna as part of their contrast therapy setup (sauna plus ice bath). This is not infrared-heated yoga - it is an infrared sauna that you use separately from classes. The distinction matters: you get the heat benefits, but not during movement.

How to use it: Book a contrast journey or sauna session after a yoga or pilates class, or as a standalone recovery treatment. The infrared sauna warms you deeply, then the ice bath closes the cycle.

Who it is for: People who want infrared heat for recovery, not during exercise. Excellent for post-surf or post-run muscle relaxation.

3. Yoga retreats in the Algarve with heated elements

Location: Various locations across the Algarve.

What they offer: Several yoga and wellness retreats in the Algarve include heated or "inferno" pilates as part of their program. These are typically multi-day residential retreats, not drop-in classes.

For example, some retreats based in Carvoeiro and the central Algarve include daily pilates and yoga alongside spa access. The pilates may be in a standard-temperature room or in a heated environment, depending on the retreat. Always check directly with the organisers before booking if heat is important to you.

Who it is for: People who want a full retreat experience with heat included. These are destination experiences, not casual drop-ins.

4. Traditional hot yoga alternatives in Lisbon

Location: Lisbon, 2.5 to 3 hours from Lagos by car or direct train.

What they offer: If you are travelling through Portugal and want hot yoga before or after the Algarve, Lisbon has several options. Core Collective and Yoga Room both offer heated flow classes. These are traditional heated rooms rather than infrared, but they provide the hot yoga experience.

Who it is for: Travellers who want hot yoga as part of a Portugal-wide trip, not specifically in the Algarve.

5. Heated hotel spas and wellness centres

Location: Various hotels across the Algarve.

What they offer: Some luxury hotels in the Algarve have heated spa facilities, saunas, and steam rooms. These provide heat for relaxation but do not offer yoga or pilates classes in the heated space. Cascade Spa at Cascade Wellness Resort near Lagos is an example - it has a Turkish bath and sauna, but classes are not part of the offering.

Who it is for: People who want heat for recovery after their own self-directed yoga or pilates practice. Bring a mat to your hotel room and use the sauna afterwards.

The difference between infrared and traditional hot yoga

If you are used to traditional hot yoga at 40 degrees Celsius, an infrared studio at 30 degrees will feel different. Here is what to expect:

Temperature: Infrared studios run 8 to 12 degrees cooler than traditional hot yoga rooms. The air is breathable. You can talk, breathe deeply, and hold poses without feeling like you are in a furnace.

Humidity: Infrared studios maintain normal humidity. Traditional hot yoga rooms often add humidity to intensify the effect, which is what makes the heat feel suffocating.

Heat depth: Infrared penetrates deeper into muscle tissue than heated air. That means your joints, fascia, and deep muscle layers warm up, not just your skin. The effect lasts longer after class.

Sweat: You will still sweat heavily in an infrared studio. The difference is that the sweat comes from internal heat, not from the air cooking you. It feels more natural.

Recovery: Infrared heat is used in physical therapy for muscle recovery. The combination of gentle heat and movement in Heat Recovery or Heat Yin classes makes it ideal for surfers, runners, and anyone with an active body.

Is infrared yoga right for you?

Infrared yoga suits people who:

  • Want the benefits of heat without the intensity of traditional hot yoga.
  • Have respiratory sensitivity and find humid, hot rooms uncomfortable.
  • Are active athletes who need recovery-focused heat rather than performance-focused heat.
  • Want deeper muscle warming for flexibility work.
  • Are beginners who find 40-degree rooms intimidating.

Infrared yoga is not right for people who:

  • Want the extreme intensity of Bikram-style hot yoga. Infrared is gentler.
  • Have conditions that contraindicate heat exposure. Pregnancy, certain medications, and cardiovascular conditions require medical clearance.

Planning your infrared yoga trip to the Algarve

If you are travelling to the Algarve specifically for infrared yoga, base yourself in Lagos. Heat Lagos is in the old town, walkable from most accommodation. The Vacation Week pass (59 EUR) covers unlimited classes for 7 days, which is the best value if you are staying for a week.

If you want to combine infrared classes with contrast therapy, Algarve Alchemy is a 5-minute walk from Heat Lagos. Book a morning class at Heat Lagos, then walk to Alchemy for a sauna and ice bath session.

For a full wellness trip, add massage from Claire Stable at Le Dome, a hike along the Ponta da Piedade cliffs, and a surf session at Porto de Mos. The combination of movement, heat, cold, and nature is what makes the Algarve a genuine wellness destination.
